\begin{abstract}
This tooling smoke study checks an offline four-second delivery measurement
against six fixed, retained baseline simulations. It measures authoritative
membership acceptance, distinguishes missing generation from delivery misses,
and records offered and sent transport costs. This is a measurement and
reproduction exercise, not a candidate evaluation or production-readiness claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and baseline}
Can a bounded workload of scheduled metadata updates be measured reproducibly
from existing evidence, without changing protocol behavior? SWIM separates
membership dissemination from failure detection~\cite{swim}; epidemic
maintenance distinguishes delay, residue and traffic~\cite{demers}.
Our four-second miss is a deadline observation, not eventual residue.
Belief-state and failure-detection measures remain separate from update
acceptance. No new dissemination algorithm is evaluated.

The baseline is the extracted Lightpool core with its own wire format, not
historical SWIM or unchanged memberlist. The complete producer/reducer revision
is pinned in \texttt{base} (prefix \texttt{36f0e38}); it changes tooling only.
Go 1.26.3 on linux/amd64 is pinned in \texttt{toolchain}. The recipe uses
\texttt{core.DefaultConfig()}, except that exchange-off sets the periodic
push/pull interval to zero. Explicit join exchanges and probe TCP fallback still run. No overlay or
candidate patch is applied.

\section{Methods and populations}
Each attempt has 32 fixed identities, empty initial metadata, joins to n000
staggered by 10 ms, uniform 1 ms latency, zero processing service time, and
1 MiB datagram/stream capacities. Full alive membership, including self,
is required just before the first event at or after 50 s. Lifetimes then
freeze. The horizon is 130 s. Sources n000 through n019 each update once,
at 50,54,\ldots,126 s, using distinct eight-byte tokens. The exact actions
are in every retained \texttt{scenario.json}.

Each cell schedules 20 updates and 620 source/receiver pairs, irrespective
of observed completions. Later suspect, dead or absent beliefs cannot remove
a pair. Generation requires a scheduled action followed by the source's
authoritative self upsert in its separate core-update event. Acceptance is
the first later authoritative receiver upsert with the source, token and an
incarnation numerically at least the generated one. Higher-incarnation
refutations retaining the token count, whatever the membership state.
Receipt alone does not count. References retain event sequence and change index.

The deadline interval is closed: generation through generation plus four
seconds. A prefix ending exactly at a deadline cannot establish a miss;
additional same-time events could still accept. A complete horizon covering
the deadline, or a strictly later prefix event, closes it. Generation failures
and incomplete generation have separate counts, never delivery misses.
Time to all receivers exists only if every frozen receiver accepts within
the deadline. Nearest-rank quantiles condition on completion; empty sets
have null quantiles.

The predeclared grid contains loss 0,10,30\%, crossed with periodic exchange
on/off, all replicate 1. Independent loss applies to every non-self directed
datagram from time zero through the closed horizon (fault end is horizon
plus 1 ns). Streams do not model independent packet loss. Protocol, fault and
schedule seeds are separate. Equal directed send ordinals pair loss draws;
traffic changes can change realized losses. No pauses, deliberate crashes,
departures or restarts are introduced. All six originals, failures included,
are retained; no outcome-based rerun or pruning selects this population.

\section{Validation and limitations}
Synthetic tests discriminate exact and just-late deadlines, same-time and
within-event ordering, duplicates, stale incarnations across digit widths,
wrong tokens/sources, generation failure, incomplete prefixes, invalid warm-up
and fixed populations despite membership exclusions. A real simulator test
suppresses UDP delivery and verifies the cited stream-message upsert and
latency. Fresh processes under GOMAXPROCS 1 and 4 yield identical evidence
and summaries, excluding host resources. Existing inspection/effect and
fresh-process replay tests are preserved. Project build, vet, lint, tidy,
unit and race checks passed.

There is one replicate per cell. Pairs are dependent within an attempt and
are not independent statistical samples. No tail estimate, monotonic-loss
claim or exchange benefit follows from this grid. The model omits ordinary
network scheduling, calibrated CPU service, TCP packet loss, encryption,
DNS and clock skew. 128-node fixture construction is tested, but no 128-node
campaign was run. The retained defaults are not a retuned research baseline.
No candidate, improvement claim or parent campaign admission is established.

\section{Reproduction}
From this checkout, run \texttt{run.sh} \texttt{NEW\_DIRECTORY} in this record to
rebuild \texttt{base} and regenerate all six rows; it refuses mismatches.
\texttt{verify.sh} \texttt{NEW\_DIRECTORY} restores originals from committed Git
history through \path{experiments/evidence.sh}, verifies exact payload
checksums and read-only offline reductions, and compares status/exit and
summary rows. Original summaries/digests are never rewritten. Failed work
remains inspectable; output paths must be new. See \path{docs/experiment-v1.md}
for the metric contract. Recreate tables with \texttt{python3 tables.py} and
render with \texttt{render.sh} \texttt{TOOL\_CACHE\_DIRECTORY}; the fixed renderer and
TeX bundle are listed in \texttt{document-toolchain}. This report is an
unreviewed agent draft and has not been released or submitted.
